# What Happens When Different Teams Need Different Views of the Same Business Data?

As businesses grow, different departments often work with the same business information but need to use it in different ways.

Sales teams may need customer details and order history, while finance teams focus on invoices and payments. Operations may need order status and task information, while management may need reports and performance summaries.

The data is connected, but the requirements are different.

## Why One View Does Not Always Work

Showing every piece of information to every employee can make business software difficult to use.

A sales employee may not need detailed financial records. A finance employee may not need the complete sales workflow. Managers may need summary-level information instead of individual transaction details.

Role-based dashboards can help present relevant information according to each user's responsibilities.

## Role-Based Access and Business Data

Role-based access allows businesses to control what different users can view or modify.

For example:

- Sales teams can manage customer information.
- Finance teams can manage invoices and payment records.
- Operations teams can manage orders and assignments.
- Managers can access reports and business summaries.

This creates a more organised way to manage user roles and permissions without maintaining separate systems for every department.

## Keeping One Source of Business Data

Different views should not create different versions of the same information.

If a customer's contact information changes, the updated record should be available wherever that information is required.

A centralised business system can maintain one source of information while presenting it differently to different users.

This can reduce duplicate data entry and inconsistencies between departments.

## Different Teams May Need Different Workflows

Different departments may also interact with the same record differently.

For example, a sales employee may create an order, a manager may approve it, operations may process it, and finance may confirm payment.

A well-planned custom business application can support these different workflows while keeping the information connected.

## Designing Software Around Business Requirements

Before developing software, businesses should identify:

- Who creates the information
- Who needs access
- Who can modify it
- Which actions require approval
- What each department needs to see
- What information management needs for reporting

This helps define software requirements before development begins.

## Key Takeaway

Different departments do not necessarily need different databases or separate software.

They often need different views, permissions, workflows, and reports built around the same business information.

Good business software should make relevant information easier to access without overwhelming users with information they do not need.
